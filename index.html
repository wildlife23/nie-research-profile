RESEARCHER PROFILE

I am a full-time researcher affiliated with the National Institute of Ecology (NIE), Republic of Korea, working in the Fish, Amphibian and Reptile Recovery Team. My research focuses on the ecology and conservation of endangered wildlife, with particular emphasis on amphibians and reptiles.

Within my individual studies, I independently develop research questions, design field and analytical methods, carry out statistical and spatial analyses, and lead manuscript preparation. These responsibilities describe my scientific role in individual studies; they do not imply formal appointment as an institutional principal investigator.

02 / Areas of expertise

Three connected research directions.

Conservation work becomes more useful when field evidence, analytical methods, and recovery planning inform one another.

01

Endangered species conservation

Field studies, habitat evaluation, captive propagation and recovery-related monitoring of threatened fauna, especially amphibians and reptiles.

02

Spatial ecology & risk modeling

Species distribution modeling, geographic information systems, statistical inference, and spatial prioritization for conservation decisions.

03

Wildlife–infrastructure interactions

Studying how wildlife interacts with human infrastructure and how ecological evidence can support risk mitigation without neglecting biodiversity.

Spatial analysis / Risk mitigation

Snake-associated electrical infrastructure incidents

Independently led research on snake-related distribution-network incidents in South Korea, combining field incident records with spatial risk modeling and external validation. Species distributions and future refugia
